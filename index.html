With Bluetooth headphones, phones often listen through the headset's own call microphone, which is far from the guitar and silences sounds that aren't a voice. If notes go missing, pick the phone's own microphone here (often called "Phone", "Built-in" or "Bottom").
Raise this if you play on time but notes count as late.
How early or late a note can be and still count.
How complete a strummed chord must sound to count. Strict: every string. Standard: most strings, but a wrong extra note counts against you. Beginner: most strings, and a brushed extra string is fine. A different chord never counts, and single notes aren't affected.
Off: just aim for the rings on the hit line. The window still works the same.
How long the app's sound takes to reach your ears. Bluetooth headphones add 150–300 ms; slide left to the end for Auto. The band, Listen and the count-in play this much early so you hear them right on the line.
Makes notes, fret numbers, chord names and strum arrows bigger.

A strip under the strings: where your hand sits, which finger plays the next note and the fingers coming up (1 index, 2 middle, 3 ring, 4 pinky, 0 open string). Works with the fretboard on or off.
After you strum a chord, ✓ and ✕ by each string show which rang, and an orange ! marks a muted string that rang. A string that can’t be heard apart from another one in the chord is left unmarked.
Uses the guitar sound above. Without headphones the phone's mic can hear the app, so “play the right note” works best with headphones.

Turn on when the app's sound goes to headphones. The mic then can't hear the band or the hit and miss sounds, so Fretline stops making allowances for them and reacts faster to your playing.

Where the sung tune for the lyrics comes from:
Chord sheets only have chords and words, so the app writes a tune that fits them. It won't be the song's real tune. If a song has a real tune, that's used first.
